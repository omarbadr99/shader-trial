Running text — the blotches land word by word¶

The ink does not arrive in a straight line. It arrives in patches, the way a plate that has been re-inked unevenly puts too much pigment in one corner of the sheet and starves the other, so a word swells into a solid lump while its neighbour stays perfectly legible.

Every word is measured against a field of noise anchored to the page rather than to the screen, which means a blotch belongs to the paragraph and not to the scroll position. Scroll back up and the same words are ruined in the same places, exactly as a misprint would be.

Two dials govern this. Unevenness decides how far a word may run ahead of or behind the bleed line. Blotch scale decides whether the ink pools in broad continents that swallow whole sentences, or scatters into a fine speckle that picks off one word in five.

Turn unevenness to zero and the effect collapses back to a clean horizontal gradient. Turn it up and the page begins to read like something recovered from a failing photocopier: dense, arbitrary, and still, just barely, readable.
